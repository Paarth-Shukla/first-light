1

Mechanistic Interpretability Needs a Logic of Inference

Zhimin Hu, Chen Jiang, Hanbo Xie, N. Apurva Ratan Murty, Sashank Varma

Desk LLMsKind position paperFiled this week

  1. As these tools improve, attention is shifting from what can be observed inside a model to how those observations support mechanistic explanations.
  2. We propose five principles and organize them into a recursive logic for a more rigorous procedure of mechanistic inference.
  3. Evidence beyond MI is ultimately required to establish what model mechanisms reveal about the world.

arXiv:2611.14483 · cs.CL

2

When does text encoding matter for language modeling?

Eugene Jang, Catherine Arnett, Terra Blevins

Desk LLMsTests 4 languagesFiled this week

  1. UTF-8’s role as the de facto encoding standard in language models introduces cross-lingual inequities due to its variable-length design.
  2. However, the effect that this encoding choice has on model performance remains largely unexamined.
  3. In light of these findings, we argue that text encoding should be a language-specific design decision, rather than a universal default.

arXiv:2611.14459 · cs.CL

3

AutoScaffold: AI-Guided Scaffolding for Reinforcement Learning of Language Models

Zijian Zhang, Rizhen Hu, Jiangweizhi Peng, Shiyang Li, Sujay Bhatt, Sihan Zeng, Mingyi Hong

Desk Machine LearningResult +7.2 pts ALFWorldFiled this week

  1. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) produces rich training experience, yet outcome rewards reduce each attempt to a single number and leave most of this information unused.
  2. We introduce AutoScaffold, a unified AI-for-AI framework that turns a student’s training experience into adaptive scaffolds, with minimal domain-specific information and no human intervention during training.
  3. The scaffolds are removed at evaluation.

arXiv:2611.14515 · cs.CL
